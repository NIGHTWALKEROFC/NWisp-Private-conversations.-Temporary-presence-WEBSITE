How presence works here

Three ideas the whole app is built around.

Your username is the whole identity

Sign up with just an email and a username — no phone number required, and your email never appears on your profile. People see a username and whatever bio you choose, nothing else.

Encrypted before it leaves your phone

Messages are encrypted on the sending device and decrypted on the receiving one, using established protocols rather than anything homemade. The server's job is to relay bytes, not read them.

Your device keeps the history, not the server

Messages pass through the server just long enough for delivery, then they're deleted — nothing is stored there long-term. Everything that matters lives on your device and the other person's, encrypted at rest, for as long as you want it there. Want a chat to clear itself automatically instead? Turn on disappearing messages for that conversation — it's off by default.

What's actually in it

Chat and Stories, done properly. Nothing else, on purpose.

Chat

  • 1:1 real-time chat — no groups yet
  • Replies, reactions, and typing indicators
  • Delivered and read receipts, online status and last-seen
  • Pin, copy, delete for yourself, or delete for everyone
  • Messages stored locally on your device, encrypted at rest
  • Disappearing messages — off by default, turn on per conversation

Stories

  • Post a Story, visible to your contacts
  • Disappears automatically after 24 hours, no exceptions

Contacts & privacy

  • Add people by username — no phone number needed to find or be found
  • Friend-style requests before someone can message you
  • Block anyone — their messages stop reaching you entirely
  • Your email is never shown on your profile

Security & appearance

  • Optional app-lock PIN on top of your device's own lock
  • Light, dark, or system theme
  • Custom accent color

Questions

Anything unclear? Probably answered here.

Is NWisp open source?

Not right now — the code is in a private repository while the app is under active, early development. That may change later.

Why build another chat app instead of using Signal or Telegram?

Both are excellent, but for different goals. NWisp is built specifically around username-first identity, messages kept encrypted on your device instead of a server, and an optional disappearing-messages toggle for chats you want to clear themselves — not trying to replace either.

Where are my messages actually stored?

On your device and the other person's, encrypted at rest — that's the copy that stays. The server only ever holds a message briefly to deliver it, then deletes its copy; it isn't a backup or an archive. If you turn on disappearing messages for a chat, the on-device copies clear too once your timer runs out.

Are messages deleted automatically?

Not unless you turn it on. By default, a conversation stays on your device the way you'd expect from any chat app — encrypted, and there until you delete it. Disappearing messages is an opt-in setting per conversation, not the default.

Can you, or the server, read my messages?

No — messages are encrypted on your device before they're sent, using established cryptographic protocols, and decrypted only on the receiving device. The server relays encrypted data; it isn't designed to read the content.

Do I need to give my phone number to sign up?

No — sign-up only needs an email and a username. There's no phone number field at all, and your email is never shown on your profile.

Does NWisp have group chats or calls?

Not yet. v1 is 1:1 chat and Stories only — no group messaging and no voice or video calls. Either might come later, but there's no promise or timeline for that right now.

Is there an iOS version planned?

Android is the only target for now. iOS isn't ruled out for later, but there's no timeline for it.

Will NWisp always be free?

Yes for v1 — free, no ads, no premium tier, nothing behind a paywall.
